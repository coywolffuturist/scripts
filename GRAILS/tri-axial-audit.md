# GRAILS — Tri-Axial Audit
*Clean report. Partner-ready. Authoritative conclusions only.*

---

## Plot Type
**Voyage and Return** — Zach is pulled out of his safe, ordered world (the shop, the Valley, the temple of authentic things) into a chaotic underworld he never sought and barely understands, and must find his way back, changed by what he saw.

Secondary engine: **Comedy** in the classical sense — misaligned worlds, mistaken assumptions, characters who think they understand the situation and don't.

---

## Zen Engine Arc
**From Fear of Change to Bold Action.**

Zach refuses the call his whole life — the shop is safe, the past is knowable, the future is terrifying. The heist is the call he can't refuse. He gets more than he bargained for, survives it, and emerges someone neither he nor Elizabeth ever expected him to become.

The grail isn't the collection. It's the man he finds out he is when the truth stops being enough and he has to act.

*Note: Zach loved Elizabeth immensely. They didn't fail from emotional distance — they failed from economics. The shop is a money pit he inherited from his father; she leaned into tech verification and the new economy while he remained anchored to the old one. Togg didn't steal her — he outbid Zach in a game Zach didn't know he was playing. Elizabeth is Zach's Grail. The heist is how he earns her.*

---

## Macro-Dopamine Forecast

- **Novelty — Act One.** The theology of sports memorabilia — grading, restoration, condition as religion — is a world most audiences have never seen. Koreatown's invisible economy is a second novelty layer. Togg's hyper-rendered Panopticon is a third. The audience is constantly arriving somewhere new.

- **Meaning — Act Two.** Once Zach realizes what he's made, the story becomes about what a man owes to his craft, his past, and the woman he lost. The heist is the pressure cooker — what's cooking is whether Zach will finally choose the living over the preserved. Elizabeth watching him change in real time is where the emotional weight concentrates.

- **Suspense — Act Three.** The heist is the climax. By then we're rooting for Jae-Wook's crew, terrified for Zach, and watching Elizabeth make the choice that defines her arc. Multiple moving pieces converging. The audience knows more than any single character. Classic Hitchcock bomb-under-the-table tension.
