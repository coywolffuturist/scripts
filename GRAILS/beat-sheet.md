### SEQUENCE 1 — First Fall (pp. 1–15)

[SEQUENCE 1 : BEAT 1 — THE FIND / COLD OPEN MONTAGE]

THE CONNECTOR: —

LOCATION: EXT. SUBURBAN GARAGE SALE → INT. KEVIN'S CAR → INT./EXT. VARIOUS → INT. ZZ HERITAGE HOUSE — DAY

CHARACTERS: Anonymous 17-year-old, Kevin, Zach (end of montage)

PLOT GOAL: Establish the MacGuffin's journey and mythic status before we meet anyone properly; land us inside the Sanctuary with Kevin already waiting
CHARACTER GOAL: Each person who touches the box wants something from it

THE ACTION: Fast-paced, kinetic. The 17-year-old at the garage sale — the find, the blank face, the price paid, the car door closes, TikTok posted, notifications erupting. Cut to Kevin — he sees the post, DMs immediately, drives to meet the kid, inspects the box with practiced eyes, pays cash. Cut to Kevin searching: the best restorer in LA, narrowing down, ZZ Heritage House. He walks in. Sets the box on Zach's counter. Zach picks it up. The montage lands — suddenly slow, reverent — on Zach's hands and the loupe.

Then the pull back: Kevin is sitting in a chair at the back of the shop, waiting. The three-generation family is at the counter with Zach — deep in the Griffey moment, the father's eyes misting, the grandfather pulled into a hug. Kevin checks his phone. Shifts in his seat. Checks again. The most important transaction of his life is being held up by an old man crying over a baseball card.

THE OPPOSITION: The box is damaged — authenticity already a question. Kevin's impatience vs. Zach's total absorption in the Griffey moment.

THE ZEN PIVOT:
Hero: Absent until the final beat — then pure connection to the object and the family
Antagonist: Absent
Sub-Protagonist: Absent — prologue; but Kevin's impatience is the first contrast with Zach's code

STAKES UPDATE: The box has arrived. Kevin is in the room. The world is about to change for Zach, though he doesn't know it yet.

ENTERTAINMENT FACTOR: Novelty (kinetic world-building, the box's journey), Suspense (who is this guy waiting?)

STRUCTURE CHECK:
- Beginning: The garage sale — the find
- Middle: The chain of hands — kid to Kevin to Zach's counter; the montage slows
- End: Kevin waiting, impatient. Zach absorbed in the Griffey moment. Title card: GRAILS

[SEQUENCE 1 : BEAT 2 — THE SANCTUARY]

THE CONNECTOR: BUT

LOCATION: INT./EXT. ZZ HERITAGE HOUSE — DAY

CHARACTERS: Zach, grandfather, father, ten-year-old son

PLOT GOAL: Establish Zach's world, his code, and make the audience root for him before the stakes arrive
CHARACTER GOAL: Do the job right — honest appraisal, no inflation, no false hope

THE ACTION:
1.1 THE AUDIT — The shop smells of acid-free plastic, aged cardboard, and the sweet chalky ghost of 40-year-old bubblegum dust. Zach, wearing a sun-faded 1984 Durham Bulls tee, sits across from three generations: a grandfather in a windbreaker, a father with tired eyes, a ten-year-old son. Between them: shoeboxes — the grandfather's hoard. Zach moves with surgical reverence. He points to the highlights: the Bill Ripken "Fuck Face" error card, the Randy Johnson Marlboro ad. The father leans in, hopeful. Zach's voice is gentle but honest: "They're 5.5s. Maybe 6 on a good day. Total value for the lot? Four, five hundred dollars." The hope leaves the father's face as he looks at his son.

1.2 THE RESTORED KING — "Wait," Zach says. He reaches under the counter and pulls out a slabbed card: the 1989 Upper Deck Ken Griffey Jr. Rookie. Crisp, centered, glowing. "Your dad brought this to me six months ago. He asked me to see what I could do." Zach explains: he cleaned the surface, pressed the fibers. "In this slab, it's a 9. But—" he turns the slab over to show the holographic "Restored" log on the back. "I logged it in the database. I won't pass off restoration as factory-original. It won't fetch the $10,000 a Natural 10 would. But it's yours. And it's perfect." The father's eyes mist as he pulls the grandfather into a silent hug. The ten-year-old watches, wide-eyed — he's never seen his gruff dad cry over cardboard before. He understands for the first time that this isn't about money. It's about time captured.

1.3 THE SANCTUARY IN THE GRAVEYARD — Zach walks the family to the door. "Take care of that Griffey." As they exit, the camera pulls back: ZZ Heritage House — warm, amber-lit — is an island in an empty strip mall. Left: a RadioShack sign hanging by a single wire over a gutted storefront. Right: a Spirit Halloween banner peeling off a defunct department store. The parking lot is cracked asphalt and weeds. Zach stands in the doorway, a solitary monk guarding a temple of relics while the rest of the world has already moved into the digital clouds.

THE OPPOSITION: The rent notice on the counter. The graveyard outside.

THE ZEN PIVOT:
Hero: Toward Connection — his code costs him (the Griffey logged as Restored, not factory-original); he doesn't hesitate
Antagonist: Absent — but the strip mall is his exhaust
Sub-Protagonist: Absent

STAKES UPDATE: We know who Zach is. We're rooting for him. The world is already moving on without him.

ENTERTAINMENT FACTOR: Novelty (the memorabilia world), Meaning (a man whose integrity is costing him everything)

STRUCTURE CHECK:
- Beginning: Three generations, the shoeboxes, the hope
- Middle: The honest appraisal — hope deflated; then the Griffey reveal
- End: The family leaves moved. Zach alone in the doorway. The graveyard behind him.

[SEQUENCE 1 : BEAT 3 — THE COMMISSION]

THE CONNECTOR: THEREFORE

LOCATION: INT. ZZ HERITAGE HOUSE — DAY

CHARACTERS: Zach, Kevin

PLOT GOAL: Establish the legitimate transaction that sets everything in motion
CHARACTER GOAL: Kevin wants a "perfect restoration as a gift for his brother" (cover story — his real intent is to use it as a counterfeit); Zach wants to do the greatest work of his life

THE ACTION: The family gone, Kevin steps to the counter. Sets the box down. Zach picks it up — and transforms. He describes what's definitely inside: the Jordan rookie, the Ewing, the Bird-Magic, the Patrick Ewing XRC. His voice goes reverent. He's already somewhere else. He offers to sell them for Kevin if that's what he's after — he's seen the industry message board posts, he knows what this is worth. Kevin says no. He wants restoration. Perfect. Like new. It's for his brother. Zach says he'd normally do this for free — this is like being asked to touch up the Mona Lisa — but there are materials, sourcing, time. Kevin asks if $10k covers it. Zach's eyes practically fall out of his head. He says yes, but that's too much. Kevin: "$5k now, $5k when you finish. But it's gotta be so perfect it makes me cry like grandpa, okay?" Zach looks at him for a moment. Extends his hand.

THE OPPOSITION: Zach's instinct to underprice himself — a lifetime habit he almost can't override

THE ZEN PIVOT:
Hero: Toward Connection — the craft, the object, the man in front of him; he shakes the hand
Sub-Protagonist (Kevin): Toward Connection on the surface — the gift story is warm and convincing; underneath, full Ego (the counterfeit scheme)
Antagonist: Absent

STAKES UPDATE: The commission is live. The box is in Zach's care. Kevin is running a counterfeit collectibles operation — robosigned Tom Brady footballs are his enterprise — and the Fleer box is his next play. Zach doesn't know any of this. We might.

// NOTE: Kevin's "it's for my brother" is a lie. His real intent is to sell the restored box as a high-end counterfeit. The dramatic irony: he hired the one man in LA whose entire code is built around never passing restoration off as factory-original. The collision between Kevin's lie and Zach's integrity is the engine that starts everything.

ENTERTAINMENT FACTOR: Novelty, Meaning (and dramatic irony on rewatch)

STRUCTURE CHECK:
- Beginning: Kevin steps to the counter; the box between them
- Middle: Zach's excitement — the descriptions, the offer to sell, Kevin's refusal
- End: The handshake. $5k changes hands. The Mona Lisa is in Zach's care.
