## The Context That Made Her

A Black woman from Inglewood. Her friends were becoming nurses, lash extension technicians, installing weaves — the realistic horizon for women from her neighborhood. She was trying to put herself through USC on a business degree. She outworked everyone. She had the mind for it.

Then AI started closing those doors before she could walk through them. The ladder got pulled up while she was still climbing. The cruelest version of the American meritocracy myth: she did everything right, and the finish line moved.

The heist isn't a moral failure. It's the only rational play left on the board.

## The Dynamic with Hye-Jin

She looks out for Hye-Jin like a big sister. Hye-Jin's empathy-bombing is a field liability — she locks onto marks and starts *feeling* for them, which threatens the operation. Deja reads the strategic picture and pulls her back. She loves Hye-Jin's gift. She also knows exactly when it becomes a vulnerability.

## Relationship to the Theme

Deja and Jae-Wook are the clear-eyed cynics. They see the world accurately. They need the softies — Hye-Jin and Zach — to make that accuracy feel worth carrying rather than a cold, heartless vacuum.

The line she gives Hye-Jin when she gets too close to the mark:
> *"Some people you have to let life fix."*

It's also what she cannot yet say to Jae-Wook about his grief.
