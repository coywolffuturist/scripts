# Jae-Wook — The Hook / The Heist Mastermind

---

## The Logline of a Person

> *"Jae-Wook is a cynic who wishes he wasn't. He'd be good if he believed goodness was still a viable option."*

---

## The Vibe

Lieutenant in a Koreatown Korean gang. The mastermind of the heist. Noble in his own code — he only steals from collectors who don't actually care about the objects they own. He never hurts people who don't deserve it. He associates with genuinely bad people. He is one of them. He knows this.

The name says it all: *Jae* (talented) + *Wook* (hook/snare). There is a trap in the name itself. He's charming enough that you forget to look for it.

---

## The Backstory That Made Him

His mother was a nurse. She worked through COVID — the whole thing, from the first wave when nobody had PPE, through the long slog when the hospitals were overwhelmed and the ICUs never emptied. She believed in it. In the work. In showing up. In the system holding.

The system did not hold.

She died. Not heroically. Just — worn down, exposed, discarded. A woman who gave everything to institutions that couldn't be bothered to give her a mask.

Jae-Wook watched that happen and made a decision: if goodness doesn't protect you, if the rules don't apply to the people writing them, then the only honest move is to stop pretending otherwise. He didn't become cruel. He became accurate.

He didn't stop wanting to be good. He stopped believing it was survivable.

---

## The Operation

Two doumis on the inside — Hye-Jin and Camille — planted in Togg's retinue after a Koreatown karaoke party. Togg assumes they're decorative. They are clocking his every move. This is the Exploit in reverse: Togg's prestige philosophy — his conviction that beautiful women are props, not actors — is the exact blindspot that gets him.

Jae-Wook designed it that way.

## Relationship to Zach

Zach is the thing Jae-Wook doesn't have a file for. A man who stayed honest — not strategically, not because he hasn't been tested, but because he actually believes in the objects and what they mean. Jae-Wook steals the same things Zach restores. They care about authenticity for the same reasons. They arrived at completely different conclusions about what to do with that knowledge.

This is the film's central tension underneath the heist: two men who understand value in a world that doesn't, trying to figure out if they're enemies.
